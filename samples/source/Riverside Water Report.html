Water Quality Report 2026

How clean our river was this year, what changed, and what we will do next.

Report RWB-2026-04Prepared by the Monitoring TeamNovember 2026

Summary

Riverside Water Board tested the river at six stations every month from January to October 2026. Water quality improved at four stations and stayed steady at two. Dissolved oxygen, the most important sign of a healthy river, stayed above the safe level of 6 mg/L all year except for two weeks of low flow in June.

Key results. Average turbidity fell by 18 percent after the new settling ponds opened in March. Nitrate stayed below the limit at every station. The Mill Creek station needs attention: its phosphate readings rose in late summer.

What we will do next

We will add weekly sampling at Mill Creek from December, meet the farms upstream about fertilizer timing, and plant a 400-meter buffer of native trees along the creek in spring. The settling ponds will be cleaned before the rainy season.

Methods

Samples were taken between 8 and 10 in the morning, kept cool and tested within six hours. Oxygen and turbidity were measured on site with calibrated meters; nitrate and phosphate were tested in the Board's laboratory. One in ten samples was tested twice to check the results.
